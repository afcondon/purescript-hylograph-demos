HYLOGRAPH-ONION

Recipes

The catalogue answers what can a mark look like. This answers how does a mark behave — the question that arrives when you are building a narrated film rather than a chart.

A watercolour blob is not one shape. It is an ordered stack of two dozen deformed variants at low opacity, and blobTree renders that stack as a keyed data join. So a wash needs no tweening and no separate animation model: transition the join’s enter phase and the layers arrive one after another. The mark paints itself, in the order a brush would lay it down.

1 Paint on

For the series: every wash reveal. Replaces a fade-in, and costs nothing extra — the layers are already there.

Each variant’s delay is staggerDelay × index, so layer 0 lands first and layer 23 last. Note the direction: the template attrs are the final state, the enter attrs the starting state.

2 Wet on wet

For the series: any frame where two things meet and the meeting is the point — in video 0, beat 7, where the wash breaches the wall.

watercolourInterleaved is not a morph between two shapes, whatever the name suggests. It alternates layer deposition between two blobs so their stacks interleave in z-order and neither sits cleanly on top: painted together while wet, rather than one over the other.

3 Drawn, not stroked

For the series: the spine, the arrows, the wall, every box rule.

A plain SVG stroke reads as mechanical against a watercolour ground. plotLine subdivides, displaces midpoints perpendicular to the segment, and overshoots each end. The overshoot is what sells it — a wobbled line with clean ends still reads as a computer being untidy; a line that runs past its corner reads as a hand.

4 Deterministic by construction

For the series: this is the one that makes a one-pass capture possible.

Seed is an Int and every generator is pure, so the film is the same film on take one and take nine. Prng.split is how you buy variety without giving that up: one seed becomes two independent streams, so a family of marks can differ while the family stays reproducible.

5 The stagger is the performance

For the series: tuning a reveal to the narration without redrawing anything.

All three are the same geometry from the same seed; only the stagger differs. A 16-second beat and a 90-second beat want visibly different deposition rates, and this is the single number that buys it — so a reveal retimes from the beat sheet without the composition being touched.

Two things worth knowing before you use these

Tick from a timer, not requestAnimationFrame. rAF fires zero callbacks in a hidden or covered tab, so a rAF-driven reveal does not slow down — it hangs forever, with no error and nothing in the console. Anything destined for a screen capture has to survive the recorder’s own window covering the tab.

Clear before you replay. A rerender against the same keys is an update, and update has no enter phase — so replaying without clearing silently does nothing at all.
